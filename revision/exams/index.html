Exams

The six theory exams, what each one is like, and mock exams to practise on. All six are multiple choice, taken online, and open book.

Key takeaways

The theory exams are sat in one week: Building Regulations and the 18th Edition on the Monday, Fundamental Inspection and Testing and PAT on the Tuesday, Periodic Inspection and Initial Verification on the Wednesday. Every exam is open book, but each allows only its own book, so know your way round it before the day. Read each question two or three times, read every answer before choosing, and never leave a question blank: answer it, flag it and come back if there's time.

Exam technique

  • Prepare the book. Tabs on the sections you use most save minutes. Tabs may only label what's on the page, and you mustn't write notes in the book, because invigilators can check it. If tabs slow you down, take them out and learn the contents and index instead.
  • Read the question two or three times. Pick out the key words. In an open book exam they tell you where to look: "electric shock" leads to basic and fault protection, for example.
  • Read every answer, even when the first one looks right. Several are often nearly right.
  • Watch for negative questions: "which is not a method of fault protection?" They're the ones most often got wrong.
  • Answer every question. If you're stuck, choose your best guess, flag it, and move on. Come back to the flagged ones at the end. A blank answer always scores nothing.
  • Don't rush, and don't get stuck. Keep an eye on the clock: in most of these exams you have about two minutes a question.

Question styles you'll meet: open questions, where the answer completes the sentence ("Basic protection can be provided by…"); closed questions, a direct question with one right answer; and negative questions, which ask for the answer that isn't true.
