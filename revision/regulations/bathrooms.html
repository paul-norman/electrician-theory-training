Regulations and compliance

Bathrooms and special locations

Water and wet skin make electric shock far more dangerous, so rooms with a bath or shower get their own rules in BS 7671 Section 701: zones, IP ratings and where sockets may go.

Key takeaways

A special location is a place of increased risk with extra rules in Part 7 of BS 7671; rooms containing a bath or shower are Section 701. Zone 0 is inside the bath or shower tray. Zone 1 is directly above it, up to 2.25 m from the finished floor (or to a fixed shower head higher than that), and includes the space under a bath if you can get at it without a tool. Zone 2 extends 0.6 m out from zone 1. A walk-in shower with no tray has no zone 2; instead its zone 1 reaches 1.2 m from the fixed water outlet. Equipment needs at least IPX7 in zone 0 and IPX4 in zones 1 and 2 (IPX5 where water jets are used for cleaning). Socket-outlets must be at least 3 m horizontally from the boundary of zone 1 (shaver supply units are the exception). Every circuit in the room needs 30 mA RCD protection.

Why bathrooms are special

Wet skin has a much lower resistance than dry skin, so the same voltage drives a bigger, more dangerous current through the body (see Ohm's law). People in a bathroom are often wet, barefoot and touching earthed metal such as taps and pipes. BS 7671 deals with this by dividing the space around a bath or shower into zones: the closer to the water, the stricter the rules.

Where water jets are used for cleaning, as in communal showers, equipment in the zones needs IPX5. And whatever the zone, every circuit serving the room, or passing through it, needs additional protection by a 30 mA RCD.

Light switches normally go outside the room, or are ceiling-mounted pull-cord switches, whose insulating cord can safely hang within reach.

IP ratings

An IP code (ingress protection) shows how well an enclosure keeps things out. The first digit is protection against solid objects (from fingers down to dust); the second is protection against water. An X means that one hasn't been tested or specified: IPX4 says nothing about solids but is splash-proof.

One catch: the water ratings for immersion (7 and 8) don't automatically include jets (5 and 6). Equipment that's been tested for both is marked with both, for example IPX5/IPX7. Separately, BS 7671 requires any enclosure around live parts to be at least IP2X (finger-proof), and its top surface at least IP4X.
