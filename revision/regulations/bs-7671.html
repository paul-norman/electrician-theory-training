Regulations and compliance

BS 7671 and its definitions

The Wiring Regulations: what they are, how they're organised, how to find your way round them, and the precise meanings they give to everyday words.

Key takeaways

BS 7671 (the IET Wiring Regulations, 18th Edition; current version BS 7671:2018+A4:2026) is the national standard for electrical installations. It's non-statutory, but meeting it is the normal way to meet the law. It has eight Parts plus appendices, and a regulation number tells you where you are: in 411.3.3, the first digit is the Part, the first two the Chapter, the first three the Section. Part 2 defines its terms precisely, and they often differ from site slang: a "fused spur" is a fused connection unit, the "earth wire" is a circuit protective conductor, and a "light fitting" is a luminaire. Basic protection stops shock in normal, fault-free conditions (insulation, enclosures). Fault protection stops shock when a single fault occurs (earthing, bonding and automatic disconnection). An RCD of 30 mA or less adds additional protection.

What BS 7671 is

BS 7671, Requirements for Electrical Installations, is the British Standard for designing, installing, inspecting and testing electrical installations. It's published jointly by the IET and the British Standards Institution, and everyone calls it "the Regs" or the 18th Edition. It's updated by amendments; the current version is BS 7671:2018+A4:2026, which means the 2018 edition with its fourth amendment, from 2026.

For the 18th Edition exam, BS 7671 itself is normally the only book you're allowed to take in, so it pays to know your way round it. Learn where things are rather than trying to memorise every value.

After the Parts come the Appendices, which hold the reference tables, such as cable current ratings and maximum earth fault loop impedances.

Examples

Numbers work like an address, getting more specific from left to right. The first digit is the Part, the first two digits the Chapter, and the first three the Section. Numbers after the first dot narrow it down to a regulation group, then an individual regulation. In Part 7, each special location has its own section: 701 is bathrooms, 702 swimming pools, 722 car charging.

Basic, fault and additional protection

BS 7671 builds protection against electric shock in layers:

  • Basic protection stops you touching live parts in normal, fault-free conditions. Examples: insulation, enclosures, barriers.
  • Fault protection protects you when a single fault occurs, such as a line conductor touching a metal case. The usual method is earthing and bonding together with a protective device that disconnects the supply quickly, called automatic disconnection of supply. See the earth fault loop explorer.
  • Additional protection is a backup in case the other two fail, or someone is careless: usually an RCD that trips at 30 mA or less.

A ceiling rose is a different thing from a luminaire: it's the accessory a pendant light hangs from. The luminaire is the complete light fitting, including everything that holds and connects the lamp.
