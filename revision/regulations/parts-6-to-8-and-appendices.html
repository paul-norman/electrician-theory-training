Parts 6 to 8 and the appendices

The rest of BS 7671: inspection and testing, the 21 special locations, prosumer installations, and the appendices you'll keep coming back to.

Key takeaways

Part 6 is inspection and testing: initial verification of new work (EIC or MEIWC) and periodic inspection of existing installations (EICR), inspecting before testing. Part 7 has 21 special-location sections; a Part 7 number is the section number in front of the general regulation it changes, and when a question names a location, look there first. Part 8 covers energy efficiency (Chapter 81, new in Amendment 4) and prosumer installations (Chapter 82). The appendices are information, not regulations: 1 standards, 2 statutory regulations and supply voltages, 3 time/current curves, 4 cable ratings and correction factors, 5 external influences, 6 model forms and the inspection checklist, 11 warning labels, 13 escape routes, 14 prospective fault current, 15 rings and radials, 16 surge protection. At the back are a table of figures, a table of tables and the index.

Part 6: inspection and testing

Every installation must be inspected and tested before it's put into service. There are only two kinds of inspection and testing:

  • Initial verification, for new work: an Electrical Installation Certificate (EIC) for a new circuit or installation, or a Minor Electrical Installation Works Certificate (MEIWC) for an alteration or addition that doesn't add a circuit.
  • Periodic inspection and testing of an existing installation, recorded on an Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR), with codes saying whether it's safe to stay in service.

Inspection always comes first, then the tests in order: continuity, insulation resistance, protection by SELV, PELV or separation, polarity, earth fault loop impedance, prospective fault current, RCD tests, phase sequence and functional checks. See Inspection and certification, Dead tests and Live tests.

Part 7: special installations or locations

Part 7 adds to or changes the general rules where the risk is higher. 701.411.3.3, for example, is the bathroom version of 411.3.3. Amendment 4 has 21 sections. The figures below are the ones that tend to come up; check each in your copy, and highlight them.

Part 8: functional requirements

Chapter 81 (new in Amendment 4) covers energy efficiency, which used to be in Appendix 17. Chapter 82 covers prosumer electrical installations (PEIs): installations that produce, store and use their own energy, with solar PV, batteries, a wind turbine or an EV, as well as a grid connection. A PEI can run connected to the grid or in island mode, cut off from it. In island mode it needs its own earth electrode, and the switching must connect neutral to earth inside the PEI without overlapping the supply's neutral. Expect this Part to grow as more homes go self-sufficient.

Revising the whole book

Don't think of it as 600 pages. Think of it as Parts, each the size of a few chapters, in the order of a job: is it in scope, what's the supply, what protection, what equipment and how to fit it, how to test it, does a special location change anything. Practise with mock questions against the clock, or plan an imaginary job (a shower, an EV charger, a garden office) and find every regulation you'd use.
